# AGPM questionnaire instruments

Text of the four rating forms used in the study (Group A and Group B, session 1 on delivery agility A1 to A5, session 2 on audit and governance G1 to G5), extracted from the Jotform forms. No responses, emails or participant data are included.

## Forms

- AGPM rating, Group A, session 1 of 2: delivery agility (A1 to A5)
- AGPM rating, Group A, session 2 of 2: IS audit and governance (G1 to G5)
- AGPM rating, Group B, session 1 of 2: delivery agility (A1 to A5)
- AGPM rating, Group B, session 2 of 2: IS audit and governance (G1 to G5)

Methods rated: Group A (11): KIBO, SAFe, Waterfall, Scrum, Spiral, DevOps, PRINCE2 Agile, XP, Scrumban, V-Model, ASD. Group B (11): DSDM, Kanban, UP/RUP, Crystal, Waterfall, LeSS, MSF, Scrum, AUP, FDD, DAD. Waterfall and Scrum were rated in both groups with identical evidence cards (verified). Method order within each criterion was fixed as in the forms.

## Session 1: consent and instructions

**Instructions (session 1)**

How to rate You rate what each method prescribes, using only the points shown for it and their sources. Match each method to the level descriptions. On a wide screen the scale stays on the left as you scroll. On a small screen it stays at the top. Do not rank the methods against each other. Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If you are unsure between two levels, choose the lower one. Aim for about 20 seconds per method. Complete the session in one sitting.

How to rate You rate what each method prescribes, using only the points shown for it and their sources. Match each method to the level descriptions. On a wide screen the scale stays on the left as you scroll. On a small screen it stays at the top. Do not rank the methods against each other. Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. ## Rating scales (identical for both groups)

### A1 Incremental delivery

Whether the method delivers in increments that each produce working software.

- 1: One delivery at the end of the project
- 2: A few large increments , planned upfront
- 3: Iterations exist, but they need not produce working, releasable software
- 4: Every iteration must produce working, releasable software
- 5: Every completed item must be usable and releasable on its own, without waiting for the iteration to end

Note shown to raters: Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If unsure between two levels, choose the lower one.

### A2 Adaptability to change

How early and how freely a changed requirement, within the agreed scope, can be taken into work.

- 1: Requirements are frozen after sign-off
- 2: Changes enter only as formal change requests , approved by a change board or sponsor
- 3: Changes enter at planned points , such as stages or increments, after an approval step
- 4: Changes enter at each iteration boundary without formal approval, and the iteration goal stays fixed
- 5: Changes can enter at any time without formal approval, including during the running iteration

Note shown to raters: Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If unsure between two levels, choose the lower one.

### A3 Release cadence

How often the method requires working software to be released, or to be releasable when no release interval is set.

- 1: One release at the end of the project
- 2: Releases more than six months apart
- 3: Releases more than one month and up to six months apart
- 4: Releases every one week to one month
- 5: Releases more often than weekly , on demand or continuously

Note shown to raters: Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If unsure between two levels, choose the lower one.

### A4 Process leanness and team autonomy

How little the method prescribes in documents and central control, and how much the team decides.

- 1: Many mandatory documents, and decisions on how to work are made centrally
- 2: Mandatory documents for each phase , and team decisions need outside approval
- 3: A moderate set of mandatory documents, and the team decides within a central plan
- 4: Few mandatory documents, and the team organizes its own work within agreed priorities
- 5: Minimal mandatory documents, and the team decides its own process and order of work

Note shown to raters: Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If unsure between two levels, choose the lower one.

### A5 Feedback integration

How often results from users, tests and operations feed back into planning.

- 1: No prescribed feedback before delivery
- 2: Feedback only at phase reviews
- 3: Periodic reviews , not tied to each increment
- 4: A prescribed review in every iteration , feeding the next iteration's plan
- 5: Continuous feedback from users, tests and operations, feeding the plan

Note shown to raters: Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If unsure between two levels, choose the lower one.

### G1 Traceability and documentation

Whether the method specifies traceability between requirements, changes, design, tests and releases.

- 1: Traceability is not mentioned
- 2: Some links , but no systematic traceability
- 3: Traceability is optional
- 4: Traceability is prescribed, but can be tailored
- 5: Full, bidirectional traceability is mandatory and kept every cycle

Note shown to raters: Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If unsure between two levels, choose the lower one.

### G2 Decision rights and accountability

Which governance decisions the method gives to named roles, and whether escalation is defined. Ordering a backlog counts as scope and priorities, not as a change decision.

- 1: No role is given decision authority
- 2: Roles are named , but their decision rights are not stated
- 3: Named roles decide on scope and priorities only
- 4: Named roles also decide on at least one of release, risk, compliance or changes
- 5: Named roles decide on release, risk, compliance and changes , with escalation defined for decisions beyond their authority

Note shown to raters: Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If unsure between two levels, choose the lower one. An element the method lists as optional rates 3 at most, and one that can be tailored away rates 4 at most.

### G3 Lifecycle control mechanisms

Whether the method sets gates, meaning defined points where a named role can approve or stop progression, such as a phase-end approval, a go or no-go decision or a change approval. Reviews without that authority, and quality checks that no one approves, are not gates.

- 1: No reviews, quality checks or gates
- 2: Reviews only , with no authority to approve or stop progression
- 3: Quality checks that work must pass, with no one approving it, such as a Definition of Done or automated tests
- 4: Gates at defined points, where a named role approves or stops progression
- 5: Gates at defined points, with each decision recorded

Note shown to raters: Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If unsure between two levels, choose the lower one. An element the method lists as optional rates 3 at most, and one that can be tailored away rates 4 at most.

### G4 Compliance integration

How the method captures, tracks and reports external obligations, such as regulation, standards, contracts and audit requirements. Managing project risks counts only where it explicitly covers these obligations.

- 1: External obligations are not mentioned
- 2: External obligations are treated as ordinary requirements or left to general organizational standards
- 3: Guidance for regulated settings is given, but no mechanism to capture and track obligations is prescribed
- 4: A prescribed mechanism captures and tracks external obligations
- 5: External obligations are captured and tracked, with compliance status reported every cycle

Note shown to raters: Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If unsure between two levels, choose the lower one. An element the method lists as optional rates 3 at most, and one that can be tailored away rates 4 at most.

### G5 Continuous audit evidence

Whether normal delivery produces audit evidence, meaning records that show controls were applied, such as approvals, test results and decision records. Ordinary documentation and progress metrics are not audit evidence.

- 1: The method does not produce identifiable evidence that can support an audit
- 2: Evidence can only be rebuilt afterwards from incidental records
- 3: Audit evidence is produced at phase ends or on request
- 4: Prescribed audit evidence is produced every iteration or release
- 5: Audit evidence is produced automatically with every change , and kept in a defined location

Note shown to raters: Choose the highest level whose description is fully met. If unsure between two levels, choose the lower one. An element the method lists as optional rates 3 at most, and one that can be tailored away rates 4 at most.
